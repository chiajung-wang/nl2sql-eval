**Step 5 follow-up — what self-correction is worth, as a controlled study.** Step 5's +0.000 gap was an *eligibility* result, not a verdict on correction: the loop only feeds back **execution errors**, and the small Step-3 slice produced none. To find where the loop earns its keep, the **same twin** was run on the frozen `step6-large-schema-retrieval-lift` slice (naive full-schema dump, up to 14 tables) as a two-axis study — first a **budget sweep** (k=3, k=5) holding the strong model fixed, then a **generator swap** (strong → weak) holding the slice fixed. The model self-corrects itself; it is never swapped between attempts.

| run | model | pass@1 | pass@k | gap | eligible exec-errors | recovered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| k=3 | `claude-sonnet-4-6` | 0.725 (29/40) | 0.725 (29/40) | +0.000 | **0** | 0 |
| k=5 | `claude-sonnet-4-6` | 0.675 (27/40) | 0.675 (27/40) | +0.000 | **0** | 0 |
| k=3 | `kimi-k2.7-code` (weak) | 0.425 (17/40) | 0.475 (19/40) | **+0.050** | **5** | 2 (+7 attempts) |

**The lever is generator weakness, not schema size or budget.** With the strong model, raising the budget from k=3 to k=5 does nothing (gap +0.000 both times) because the eligible execution-error population is **0** — *every* pass@1 failure (11, then 13) was a **wrong-answer** (valid SQL, wrong result), not one execution error. The big schema didn't help: a capable model writes *syntactically valid* SQL even against 14 tables; it just sometimes selects the wrong valid columns, and error feedback can't fix a semantic mistake. Swap in the **weaker** `kimi-k2.7-code` on the *same* slice and the loop finally has fuel: **5** attempt-1 execution errors, of which it recovers **2** — pass@1 0.425 → pass@3 **0.475**, a **+0.050** gap, bought with **7 extra generate→execute cycles** and ~94s added wall-clock. (Added *dollar* cost is unpriced here: `kimi` routes via OpenRouter, whose cost the dated list table the twin prices from doesn't carry — the cross-provider table reconciles that via provider-reported cost; #52.)

**Why the +0.050 is signal, not the noise floor — and what it is *not*.** This is the distinction the twin metric exists to make. The strong-model 0.725-vs-0.675 difference is **between two independent runs**, so it is pure resampling jitter (~0.05 floor, temperature>0). The weak-model +0.050 is **within one run**: pass@1 is derived from the *same* pass@3 run's first attempts, so the 2 recovered questions are specifically ones that **errored on attempt 1 and the correction loop flipped to correct** — `recovery_rate` and the **+7 added attempts** are the causal proof the loop did the work, not a second sample. That said, this is **one weak-model run at one k (n=1)**: it is a clean *demonstration that the loop fires and recovers*, not a precise estimate of the magnitude — the **+0.050** is illustrative, and a fuller claim would need replication and a k-sweep on the weak model. The honest synthesis stands: **self-correction is worth roughly the execution-error rate × the recovery rate**, so its value rises with how malformed the generator's SQL is — near-zero for a strong model (0 errors to feed back), demonstrably non-zero for a weak one — and the twin reports that lift *with its retry price*, never as a bare headline. **Step 11 (#121) — robust `_extract_sql`, and the truncation it uncovered.** The generate-stage SQL extractor was anchored to the *whole reply* (`^…```…```…$`), so a reasoning model that wraps its SQL in chain-of-thought prose had the entire blob dropped through unparsed → `guard` rejects it. **Step 12 (#139) — deterministic soundness checks: a measured lever with a clean, non-model mechanism.** Where the table-selection cluster (#122) is a model-capability wall, the paper's (arXiv:2505.19988v2 §4) *bad-construction* checks are concrete, mechanical wrong-answer shapes a deterministic check **can** catch — the rare Step-12 lever with a non-model mechanism. Three sqlglot-AST checks land in a new `soundness` stage (`src/nl2sql/pipeline/soundness.py`), same shape as the guard but a **correction** contract, not a safety gate: `null_ordering` (`min(f)` / `ORDER BY f ASC LIMIT` over an unguarded column — a NULL sorts first, so the result is NULL-driven wrong), `minmax_subquery` (`= (SELECT MIN/MAX(x) …)` where `ORDER BY x LIMIT 1` is idiomatic), and `field_catenation` (a projection fusing two+ distinct fields). The primary, offline deliverable is the fixture proof (PRD rule 11): on `fixtures/soundness/` the checks score **catch rate 1.000 (9/9)** with **false-positive rate 0.000 (0/12)** over curated positives + near-miss negatives (a guarded `min`, `MAX`/`DESC`, a full ordered list, a *correlated* min/max subquery, a column-plus-literal concat). **Honest caveat on that number:** the `min(f)`-projection positives encode the paper's/spec's claim that a bare `min(f)` without a `NOT NULL` guard is a hazard — which is *debatable* in isolation, since SQL `MIN` ignores NULLs in aggregation (`SELECT MIN(price)` returns the minimum non-null price). The check earns its keep on the BIRD questions where the gold query itself adds `… IS NOT NULL` (the column's NULLs mean "missing", and the intended answer excludes them), so it mirrors gold behaviour rather than an objective SQL truth; the `ORDER BY f ASC LIMIT` positives, by contrast, are an unambiguous wrong-row hazard. So the `9/9` is a catch rate against a *spec-inherited* ground truth for ~3 of the 9, not a claim that all nine are objectively wrong SQL — the honest reading the live A/B (deferred) would adjudicate. Wiring decision, documented and tested: a soundness hit is a **correction signal, never a hard reject** — with retry budget left the graph feeds the reason back to `generate` (via `correct_soundness`, the existing feedback channel); with the budget spent the flagged candidate **proceeds to execute anyway**, so a false positive costs at most a wasted retry, never a dropped answer (a soundness heuristic must never *lose* a run). The checks judge the **outermost query** — the result the user receives — so a scalar `MIN(x)` building-block subquery (a correct idiom) is never flagged. The existing guard red-team set (43/43) and 582 prior tests stay green. **Deferred (gated on a key/spend — [[defer-api-key-verification]]):** the live dev A/B pass@1 (± the checks) and the `diagnose_bird` bucket movement; on the flash-class generators the precision buckets are already near-zero (#117), so the live headline lift may be ~0 even at a perfect fixture catch rate — an acceptable, documented outcome, since the durable result is the fixture catch/FP rate. Reproduce the offline proof: `uv run pytest tests/test_soundness.py -s` (prints the catch/FP rates).

**Step 12 (#140) — profiling-derived field metadata: the offline machinery built; the accuracy A/B deferred.** The paper's (arXiv:2505.19988v2 §2.1) most surprising result is that **profiling the data beats human-supplied metadata** (MiniDev, no hints: profiling 61.2 vs supplied 59.6; fused best 63.2), because cryptic schemas hide format the data exposes. Step 11 ruled out the cheaper metadata moves (FK enrichment #112 was a null — the FKs were already in the DDL) but never tried **data-derived** field descriptions; this builds the whole offline machinery for that lever, split by the determinism boundary (CLAUDE.md §4): (1) a **deterministic profiler** (`src/nl2sql/profiling/profiler.py`) — per-column counts, NULL/non-NULL, distinct, value shape (length, character class, common prefix, min/max), top-k — over the SQLAlchemy executor; (2) a **mechanical** profile→English renderer (no LLM); (3) an **offline, cached** LLM summarization (short for linking #138, long for generation) frozen to a version-controlled `profiles/<db>.json` artifact the live pipeline only *reads*; and (4) a **metadata-source selector** (`METADATA_SOURCE` = supplied / profiling / fused) that rides the chosen descriptions into the rendered schema via `build_schema_index(column_descriptions=…)` — wired into the BIRD eval's index-build (`eval/eval_bird_rag.py`), so the env axis is *active* in a real run (default `supplied`, or no artifact, is byte-identical to before). Two **load-bearing boundaries**, both enforced structurally not by prose: these descriptions are *generate-prompt content only* — never read by `guard.py` or `eval/compare.py`, which stay deterministic and data-independent, so scoring is untouched; and profiling is **PII-aware** — a redaction-policy column is profiled *shape-only* (counts, distinct, length, character class), never its raw values (no `min`/`max`/prefix/top-values), so raw PII can't reach the committed artifact, the prompt, or a trace (CLAUDE.md §5.3) on the payments path. Proven offline (35 tests): the profiler recovers the paper's own examples — a 14-char all-digit `CDSCode` renders as *"exactly 14 characters long, all digits, always beginning with 0110017000000, unique"*; a `'YYYY-YYYY'` academic year as *"exactly 9 characters long … ranges 2013-2014 to 2014-2015"* — and the summarizer parses the model's short+long JSON (degrading to the deterministic English on a malformed reply), the cache round-trips, and the selector fuses supplied+profiling identically for the harness and demo. **No accuracy number is claimed yet** — this is the largest build in the set, and the headline is the three-way A/B. **Deferred (gated on a key/spend — [[defer-api-key-verification]]):** the live summarization precompute over the real BIRD dbs, and the **supplied vs profiling vs fused** dev A/B (pass@1 strict + BIRD, per-question prompt-token cost, `diagnose_bird` bucket movement, held-out headline) — this replicates or refutes the paper's *profiling > supplied* finding on our slice, reported plainly either way. Reproduce the offline machinery: `uv run pytest tests/test_profiling.py`; inspect the deterministic English for any db with `uv run python -m nl2sql.profiling.summarize --db-url <url> --db-id <db> --dry-run`.

**Step 12 (#141) — literal→field steering: the deterministic machinery for "right value, wrong column".** A recurring wrong-answer class (the `ambiguous_column` root cause #134 labels) is a literal constrained against a plausible-but-wrong field. The paper (arXiv:2505.19988v2 §3) handles it deterministically: index sampled field values, then after generation check whether each literal actually occurs in the column it's constrained against; if not, name the columns that *do* contain it and ask the model to rephrase — their worked example flips a `County Name` constraint to the correct `District` field. This builds that whole machinery, offline and deterministic: a **sampled value index** (`src/nl2sql/value_index.py`) mapping a normalized value → the columns whose sample holds it (a *moderate* sample, not full-column — the paper's scalability caution — and **PII columns are never indexed**, mirroring #140's redaction boundary); a **post-generation literal check** (`src/nl2sql/pipeline/literal_check.py`) that extracts `col = 'x'` / `col IN (…)` bindings by **sqlglot AST** (never a regex — the determinism rule), resolves the column (alias-aware), and — only when *confident* (the constrained column was sampled and lacks the literal while another column holds it) — emits a steering finding; and a `literal_check` **stage** wired as a **correction signal** (like #139's soundness): a flag with budget left feeds the steering message back to `generate` via `correct_literal`, a flag with the budget spent executes anyway (a false steer costs at most a wasted retry, never a dropped run). Selectable via the `LITERAL_STEER` env axis, off by default (a default run is byte-identical). Proven offline (20 tests): the check **recovers the paper's `CountyName`→`District` flip** and its alias-qualified form, stays silent on an on-column literal and on an unknown literal (no false steer), reads `IN`-clause literals, and the graph feeds an off-column literal back and regenerates to the corrected candidate. **No accuracy number is claimed yet.** **Deferred (gated on a key/spend — [[defer-api-key-verification]]):** the live dev A/B (pass@1 strict + BIRD), the `diagnose_bird` `ambiguous_column`/`where_mismatch` subset movement, and the **trigger rate / recovery rate / false-steer rate** + added-retry cost — the Step-5-twin pattern of value-with-its-price. **One honest simplification:** the index uses **exact normalized (casefold + strip) match**, not the paper's LSH-on-shingles approximate match — so a near-miss literal (a typo, different punctuation) won't match a sampled value. That's the conservative direction (it under-triggers rather than false-steers), and it fits BIRD's exact string literals; approximate/LSH matching is a documented later extension. Reproduce the offline proof: `uv run pytest tests/test_literal_field.py`.

**Step 12 (#142) — result-set majority voting: the comparator, repurposed as a candidate selector.** The paper's submission (arXiv:2505.19988v2 §4) generates several candidates and selects one by **majority vote on executed result-sets**. The clean reuse: **our comparator (`eval/compare.py`) *is* the result-set equivalence that vote needs** — including BIRD set-semantics — so voting is the proven comparator applied to *candidate selection* instead of *gold scoring*; no new SQL-equivalence logic, no string-match, no LLM judge (CLAUDE.md §7). `eval/voting.py` groups k candidates into result-set equivalence classes via `compare(a, b, "SELECT 1").correct` (an order-insignificant sentinel — "convert results to sets"), picks the largest class, and breaks a tie or no-majority **deterministically by earliest candidate index** (the paper picks randomly; a reproducible tiebreak is required by §9). Errored candidates are excluded; if all errored, the first is returned so a run is never lost. Candidate diversity is the paper's schema-field-order randomization (`eval/candidate_diversity.py` — a seeded, deterministic permutation of each table's columns; seed variation is the live-only second lever, since it needs a stochastic model). **The scoring boundary is untouched:** voting only *chooses* which candidate's **raw verified result** the harness scores against gold, upstream of redaction (§3/§5) — `select_by_vote` returns the run, never a score. Proven offline (21 tests): unanimous / 2-of-3 majority / no-majority-tiebreak, tie-between-two-pairs, errored-exclusion, order-insensitive **and** float-tolerance equivalence (the comparator rules, reused), the agreement distribution, `run_voted` end-to-end, and the field-order shuffle's determinism/permutation/seed-0-identity. **No accuracy number is claimed yet** — and per Step 5's honest framing, an honest null (a strong generator whose candidates agree → vote is a no-op, +0.000) is an *expected, acceptable* outcome; the finding is *where* voting beats attempt-1. **Deferred (gated on a key/spend — [[defer-api-key-verification]]):** the live twin **pass@1 (attempt-1) vs pass@k (majority-selected)** on a strong (`accuracy`) **and** a weak generator, the vote-agreement distribution over the slice, the k× generation/execution cost, and the comparison against the self-correct selector at the same budget. Reproduce the offline proof: `uv run pytest tests/test_voting.py`.
