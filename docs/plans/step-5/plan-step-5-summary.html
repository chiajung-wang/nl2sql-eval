Plan · Step 5 · Measured features

Self-correction (execution-error feedback) + pass@k

Phase: Measured features Depends on: Step 3 (harness/pass@1) · Step 4 (guard gate) Unblocks: Step 6 (retrieval re-trigger hooks into this loop)

1 Headline & goal

Quantify what self-correction is actually worth.

Turn the single-shot generator into an agent by adding a correction loop driven by execution-error feedback only, and extend the harness to report pass@1 AND pass@k. The gap between them is a headline finding — "self-correction recovers X% of initial failures at a cost of Y% added latency" (CLAUDE.md §5/§6, PRD §9). Recovery must never look free: the same run also reports attempts, cost, and latency.

1 new stage — correct.py
1 capped retry budget (configurable)
2 metrics — pass@1 + pass@k
1 of the two sharpest findings

2 What to build

pipeline/correct.py — the correction stage

  • On execution failure (syntax/runtime error), capture the error message and feed it back as a correction signal that re-triggers generate.
  • Capped retry budget — prevents infinite loops and runaway cost; the cap is itself a cost/latency lever to discuss.
  • Execution-error feedback only — retrieval re-trigger is deferred to Step 6.

pipeline/graph.py — the loop-back edge

  • Conditional edge execute → correct → generate, governed by the budget.
  • Terminal transitions: recovered → success; budget spent → retry_exhausted; final error → execution_error_final.
  • Enum lives in state.py; the classifier stays in the harness (CLAUDE.md §3).

Harness extension — twin metrics

  • pass@1: generator alone, no correction.
  • pass@k: same questions, full correction budget.
  • Per-question attempts, cost, latency — so the recovery is not free in the numbers.

pass@1 reads the first attempt only; pass@k reads success within the capped budget. The correction stage feeds execution errors back into generation — but never re-triggers retrieval (that is Step 6). Every run also reports attempts/cost/latency so recovery is never free.

5 Done when & results log

Done when: you can state the pass@1 → pass@k gap — e.g. "self-correction recovers X% of initial failures at a cost of Y% added latency."

Append to RESULTS.md: pass@1, pass@k, the gap, and the added cost/latency with model, slice ID, prompt version, date, commit. This is one of the two sharpest findings — capture it now, don't reconstruct it later (CLAUDE.md §6).

6 Pitfalls

PitfallSeverityWhy it bites
Silent retries masking problems narrative Retries that eventually stumble into the right answer make accuracy look good while latency/cost quietly balloon. Reporting both numbers and cost is the guard against this.
Uncapped / hard-coded budget cost Keep the retry cap explicit and configurable — it's a cost/latency lever, not a magic constant.
Re-triggering retrieval here ordering Schema-RAG doesn't exist until Step 6; this loop handles execution-error feedback only. The not-found re-retrieve is a Step 6 contribution.
Classifier creeping into state.py integrity The enum lives in state.py; the terminal-state classifier stays in the harness (CLAUDE.md §3).

7 Guardrails for this step

Never (anti-patterns)

  • Let the correction loop run uncapped — runaway cost and infinite loops.
  • Re-trigger retrieval here — that's Step 6 (corrected ordering).
  • Report pass@k without its cost/latency — recovery must not look free.
  • Put the terminal-state classifier in state.py.

Always

  • Report both pass@1 and pass@k, plus the gap.
  • Keep the retry budget explicit and configurable.
  • Capture attempts, cost, latency per question.
  • Validate the loop on payments gold before BIRD.
