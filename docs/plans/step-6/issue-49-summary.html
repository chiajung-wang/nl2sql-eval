1 Overview

The Step-6 Definition of Done: measure the naive-dump → schema-RAG retrieval lift on a frozen large-schema slice — and report it honestly, even when it's negative.

The same import-shared pipeline runs over a frozen, seeded, stratified large-schema slice (40 questions, disjoint from the Step-3 small-schema slice) twice: once with the Step-3 naive full-schema dump, once with schema-RAG. The lift is the accuracy delta; the retrieval-recall metric (#47) explains it.

This is the second of the project's two sharpest findings — and like Step 5's pass@k gap, the measurement apparatus earns its keep by surfacing the real result rather than a hoped-for headline.

-0.125 retrieval lift
0.942 retrieval recall
40 frozen large-schema questions
367 tests green

2 The number — and why it's negative

naive 0.700  →  schema-RAG 0.575  ·  lift −0.125

Schema-RAG lowered pass@1 by 12.5 points (28/40 → 23/40). The recall metric diagnoses it directly:

SignalValueWhat it means
naive pass@10.700 (28/40)The full dump hands the model every table — and these dbs (≤14 tables) fit the context.
schema-RAG pass@10.575 (23/40)RAG's job is to drop tables to fit a budget; on a schema that already fits, that can only lose information.
retrieval recall0.942~5.8% of gold tables were missed (concentrated in the 10–14-table dbs) → those become silent wrong answers.

Retrieval is not free. Its lift appears where the schema overflows the prompt; on these BIRD dev dbs it doesn't, so retrieval only costs. The honest reading is the deliverable — the DoD is to measure the lift, not to win it.
